Snowboarding Fundamentals

A short course that takes a rider who can skid down a slope and turns them into one who can link turns down a green run and keep up with the group on a trip. Eight tightly-scoped lessons, grounded in the official instructor standards.

The mission

A snowboarding trip is coming up, and the goal is to keep up with friends and family on the mountain — riding the same runs, at their pace, instead of trailing behind. Everything here points at that. The bottleneck is turning, so the whole course builds toward linking turns.

Lessons

Work through them in order — each one is a prerequisite for the next. Roughly four hours in total, best spread across several sessions at the snowdome.

  1. 01 How a Snowboard Actually TurnsThe mental model: edge, pressure, rotation — and the three phases of every turn. ≈20 min
  2. 02 Stance & Balance: The Athletic PositionThe home position everything is built on. Soft legs, upright torso, hips over the board. ≈30 min
  3. 03 Edging & the SideslipThe grip dial: heel and toe sideslip, edge angle, and how not to catch an edge. ≈35 min
  4. 04 Pressure: Fore/Aft & Flexion–ExtensionWhere you push and how hard — the dial that shapes a turn. Plus the ski-pole drill. ≈30 min
  5. 05 Rotation: Knee SteeringThe movement that replaces counter-rotation. Front knee across, back knee follows. ≈30 min
  6. 06 Garlands & the Falling LeafTurning without changing edges — the bridge that makes your first real turns easy. ≈30 min
  7. 07 Your First Turns: C-Turns to Linked S-TurnsThe payoff. Assemble everything into real turns and link them down a green. ≈40 min
  8. 08 On the Mountain: Speed, Terrain, Lifts & the GroupRiding lifts, choosing terrain, the Responsibility Code, and keeping up with your group. ≈30 min

How to use this course

  • Space it out. The goal is long-term retention, not a single good day. Do a lesson, practise it at the dome, and let a few days pass before the next. Spacing beats cramming.
  • Read, then do. Every lesson has a Do this at the dome box. The reading is short; the practice is where the skill lives.
  • Use the glossary. The glossary is the canonical vocabulary for this course. When a lesson names a concept, it means the definition there.
  • Ask your teacher. This was written by an agent that is still available to you. Confused, stuck, or curious? Ask — every lesson ends with a note saying so.
  • Cite the sources. Every claim links to its source: the AASI/PSIA instructor standards, the CASI beginner system, and named certified instructors. Nothing here is guesswork.
