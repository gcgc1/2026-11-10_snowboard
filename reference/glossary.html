Snowboarding Glossary

The vocabulary this course uses, compressed for quick reference. When a lesson names a concept, it means the definition here — not a loose synonym.

The board & your stance

Regular / Goofy
Which foot leads. Regular = left foot forward (most riders); goofy = right foot forward. Nose (tip) is the leading end, tail the trailing end.
Toe edge / Heel edge
The two metal edges. The toe edge is under your toes; the heel edge is under your heels. You are always on one or the other (or momentarily flat).
Fall line
The straightest, steepest line down the slope — the direction gravity pulls you. A board pointing down the fall line accelerates; across it, you slow.
Athletic (neutral) stance
Ankles, knees and hips softly flexed, torso upright, shoulders and hips roughly aligned with the board. The home position everything returns to.

How a turn works — the three dials

Edging (tilt)
How far you tilt the board onto its edge. More edge angle = more grip and braking; flat = sliding. Avoid: "leaning" — edging is the board's angle, not your torso's.
Pressure
How much, and where, you push into the board: forward/back along its length (fore/aft), side-to-side across its width, and the raw magnitude of it.
Rotation
How you twist and guide the board with your legs and body — steering it around its length.
Counter-rotation
Twisting the upper body one way while the board goes the other (classically, swinging the back foot to turn). Works only at slow speeds and then breaks down. Avoid: "hip steering", "throwing the back foot".
Knee steering
Steering the board by pulling your front knee across over it, with the back knee following a beat later — front lever first, back lever second. The movement that replaces counter-rotation.
Inclination / Angulation
Two ways to get the board on edge. Inclination = leaning your whole body into the turn; angulation = bending at the ankles/knees/hips to increase edge angle with a more upright body.

The three phases of a turn

Initiation (the edge change)
The start of a turn: you release the edge you are on and let your centre of mass cross over the board onto the new side. Every turn starts here, not at the nose.
Control (the apex)
The middle phase, passing through the fall line. You manage edge and pressure so the board arcs where you want rather than being dragged by gravity.
Completion (the finish)
The board finishes across the slope, pressure builds and you slow — then it becomes the initiation of the next turn.

Edging & sliding drills

Sideslip
Sliding sideways down the fall line on one edge, board across the slope. Heel-side = facing downhill, on the heel edge; toe-side = facing uphill, on the toe edge. Low edge angle glides, high edge angle brakes.
Traverse
Sliding across the slope on an edge, travelling along the hill rather than down it.
Falling leaf
Sliding down on one edge while sweeping back and forth — nose to the left, then to the right, then back — without changing edges. Named for the leaf's path.
Garland
Following the shape of a turn but flattening and re-engaging the same edge, so you never actually change edges. The bridge between sideslipping and real turns.

Turning outcomes

C-turn / J-turn
One single turn, from across the slope, through the fall line, back across the slope — shaped like a C (or a J at the end). Your first real turn.
Linked turns / S-turns
C-turns joined together, alternating edges, so the tracks draw S shapes down the hill. The goal of this course.
Skidded turn
A turn where the board slides sideways as it goes — forgiving and slow. What beginners (and most recreational riders) do.
Carved turn
A turn where the edge grips and the board slices cleanly with no sideways skid. The long-term goal; out of scope for now.
